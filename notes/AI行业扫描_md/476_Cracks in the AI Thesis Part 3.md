## **Model adoption: have we hit the top of the S-curve?**

In September, OpenAI narrowed Anthropic’s lead in business AI adoption. Among Ramp businesses, **43.8% paid Anthropic**, up 0.25 percentage points from last month. OpenAI adoption rose **1.12 percentage points to 40.6%**, outpacing overall AI adoption. Overall adoption reached **55.9%**, increasing **0.62** percentage points, slightly faster than August’s **0.40** point gain.

I sometimes question the value of this metric. It mattered when AI was new and OpenAI dominated the market, but today, more than half of businesses pay for AI. Many of them pay for multiple models.

And today, the growth in new AI customers has slowed down significantly. It feels like we’re at the top of the S-curve, where the growth in the market will have to come from more intense adoption rather than more customers.

Individual modem companies can still win share. We see outsized growth for xAI, a small player but up 0.6 percentage points on the popularity of Grok Bot. Mistral AI lags the market at less than 1% adoption by U.S. firms, which indicates low demand for the French AI company, even amongst multinational firms with a European presence.

## **Per employee AI spend stutters, rising, but still below its highs in July**

We [previously](https://x.com/arakharazian/status/2095204452609171555) shared that 80% of spend on OpenAI and Anthropic is driven by 1% of firms. On a per-employee basis, spend is stuttering for the top 1% of firms. Although per-employee spend rose **11.7%** from last month, the top 1% of firms spent **$9,568 per employee per month** in September, **down** **3.1%** fromits July high of **$9,873**.

**Methodological note**: Because the top 1% represents a small group of firms, its spending estimate is more volatile than our median and top 10% estimates. We also revised up spending for the top 1% across cohorts in all periods after adding new AI vendors to our estimates. These results are subject to change.

It’s clear this effect is not seasonal. Token volumes, for example, rose over the same period. It’s token spend that led to these declines. And this slowdown has been unusually stubborn. **Because of August and September, we’ve recorded two straight months of virtually no growth in token spend.**

## **What about contracts?**

A few of you have written me to ask if OpenAI and Anthropic are asking customers to sign contracts to lock-in enterprise spend. Based on what I’ve heard from businesses who have signed or seen those deals, the customer is in the favorable negotiating position. I have heard that the contracts are 1) rarely signed in a manner that assumes a higher-than-normal AI spend on the part of the customer, and 2) to the extent they are, the labs are willing to provide concessions in the form of discounts on models from the sticker price. In other words, the contracts are not particularly restrictive. These contracts could contribute to price cuts without driving additional volume.
